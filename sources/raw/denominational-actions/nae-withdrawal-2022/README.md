# PCA and the National Association of Evangelicals: withdrawal controversy

This dossier treats the 2022 withdrawal as the culmination of a recurring PCA controversy rather than an isolated vote.

## Denominational affiliation chronology

Official PCA sources report:

- 1973: the First General Assembly approved Mission to the World's affiliation with NAE-related bodies/services.
- 1986: the 14th General Assembly approved full PCA membership in the National Association of Evangelicals.
- 2011: Central Carolina Presbytery sent Overture 2011-12 asking the 39th GA to withdraw from the NAE. The Assembly referred the question to Interchurch Relations for study.
- 2012: the 40th GA answered the Central Carolina overture in the negative and instructed Interchurch Relations to monitor and report significant NAE actions/positions.
- 2013: the Assembly took exception to an overly narrow interpretation of that monitoring mandate.
- 2019: Central Carolina again sent an NAE-withdrawal overture, Overture 23. The Interchurch Relations Permanent Committee recommended remaining; the Committee of Commissioners recommended withdrawal; the Assembly again declined to withdraw.
- 2022: Overture 3 from Pee Dee Presbytery finally resulted in PCA withdrawal.

## Verified 2022 event facts

- Overture 3 from Pee Dee Presbytery proposed that the PCA withdraw from the National Association of Evangelicals.
- The overture cited NAE advocacy concerning environment/stewardship, immigration, the death penalty, and the `Fairness for All` approach to sexual orientation, gender identity, and religious freedom, while acknowledging that Christians may legitimately disagree about public policy approaches to stewardship, immigration, and civil justice.
- The Interchurch Relations Permanent Committee recommended answering Overture 3 in the negative.
- The Committee of Commissioners offered a substitute recommending that Overture 3 be answered in the affirmative.
- TE Kenneth McHeard chaired the Committee of Commissioners presentation.
- TE L. Roy Taylor chaired the Permanent Committee and argued for continued membership.
- TE Carl Robbins argued for withdrawal.
- TE Rhett Dodson also delivered a floor speech supporting withdrawal; Presbyterian Polity preserves a linked recording/summary.
- The Assembly first recorded a 1030–699 vote approving the substitute. After a point of order concerning whether a separate main-motion vote was required, the Moderator called a revote. The final controlling vote was **1059–681** to answer Overture 3 in the affirmative and withdraw from the NAE.
- Dr. Walter Kim, then president of the NAE, provided fraternal greetings by video during the Assembly.
- TE Roy Taylor subsequently offered a formal protest against withdrawal. The protest stated, among other grounds, that withdrawal cast doubt on the PCA's practice of catholicity.
- The Minutes preserve a long named roster of commissioners who joined that protest. This roster should be normalized person-by-person as its own dataset.

## 2019 National Partnership connection

The uploaded National Partnership archive documents explicit internal opposition to withdrawal in 2019:

- Bruce O'Neil, writing as a member of the Interchurch Relations Committee, argued that continued NAE membership gave the PCA theological influence in a broader evangelical body and noted that Roy Taylor had chaired the NAE board for more than a decade.
- James Kessler wrote that he agreed completely with O'Neil's perspective.
- Kessler repeatedly identified defeating the proposed withdrawal as an Assembly goal and urged NP members to be present for the floor vote.

This is a direct organization-to-issue connection. It should not be generalized to every NP member's personal view without individual evidence.

## Walter Kim trajectory note

The same 49th GA minutes list Walter Kim among ministers dismissed to another denomination in 2021: Blue Ridge Presbytery, 2021-09-01, to the Conservative Congregational Christian Conference (CCCC). This is useful for trajectory/current-status modeling and should remain distinct from his role as NAE president.

## Why this source family matters for other issue maps

The NAE controversy intersects several issues the broader project is tracking because the withdrawal overtures themselves mention:

- immigration/public policy;
- environmental/stewardship advocacy;
- capital punishment;
- sexual orientation and gender identity / Fairness for All;
- church-state / WCF 31.4 boundaries;
- ecumenical/catholic evangelical cooperation.

This lets the site distinguish people who supported continued ecumenical affiliation from those who supported withdrawal without pretending the disagreement was reducible to a single social issue.
