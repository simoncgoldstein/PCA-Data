## Institutional status

MTW identifies itself as the global missions-sending agency of the Presbyterian Church in America.

## Current coordinator

- Dr. Lloyd Kim — MTW Coordinator, appointed after Paul Kooistra's 2014 retirement; official history describes his tenure as beginning in 2015.

### Lloyd Kim education and ministry trajectory from official MTW biography

- B.A./undergraduate engineering — University of California, Berkeley
- M.Div. — Westminster Seminary California
- doctorate in New Testament Studies — Fuller Theological Seminary
- former associate pastor — New Life Mission Church (PCA), Fullerton, California
- MTW missionary beginning in 2004 in the Philippines and Cambodia
- later MTW field leadership
- called as MTW Coordinator in 2014 / serving as Coordinator from 2015

These are direct examples of the career/education edges the project should preserve for Tier A people.

## Strategic initiatives under Lloyd Kim

MTW's official history says that under Lloyd Kim, the agency adopted three strategic initiatives:

1. **Globalization** — expanding gospel-centered church-planting partnerships globally
2. **Diversification** — sending a more diverse missionary force
3. **Mobilization** — sending more people as cross-cultural missionaries

The current About page also promotes a need for multicultural missionaries and references `Diversifi` and `RADD (Reformed And Diverse Delegates)` as initiatives/resources connected to multicultural mobilization.

These should be represented as MTW program/strategy nodes, not as personal ideology labels.

## Mercy, justice, race and refugee-related institutional language

MTW's current About page includes `Mercy, Justice, & the Love of God` among institutional-value headings and describes ministries flowing from churches into community transformation, including refugee service.

In a July 2019 Coordinator essay, Lloyd Kim writes that MTW's church-centered mission does not exclude justice and mercy and explicitly includes care for strangers, economic justice and racial justice among outworkings of Christian mission.

In an October 2018 essay, Kim explains MTW's emphasis on missionary diversity as flowing from the multiethnic kingdom of God and as strengthening the church's witness.

These sources are useful issue-position records for MTW leadership and strategy. They should not automatically be attributed to every MTW missionary.

## Scale / privacy rule

The current MTW About page reports hundreds of long-term missionaries plus large numbers of shorter-term missionaries, interns, participants and national partners across roughly 100 countries.

The project should **not indiscriminately ingest every missionary** merely because a name may be public. Initial scope should be:

- Coordinator and executive leadership;
- public-facing senior staff;
- regional directors;
- named program/strategy leaders;
- people who independently recur in other high-value PCA datasets;
- public current biographies relevant to institutional trajectories.

This provides institutional coverage without creating unnecessary personal-data collection on missionaries whose names add little analytical value.
