# Revoice / homosexuality / Side-B controversy in the PCA

Snapshot/update: 2026-09-03

This dossier treats Revoice as a multi-year PCA controversy with distinct event types rather than a single ideological label. Hosting a conference, serving on a presbytery committee, signing a protest, writing a minority report, serving on the Human Sexuality AIC, or participating in later SJC litigation are separate edges.

## 2018: first Revoice conference at Memorial Presbyterian Church

Primary later judicial summary:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/49th_pcaga_2022_vol02.pdf

SJC Case 2020-05 states that Memorial Presbyterian Church in St. Louis hosted the first Revoice Conference in July 2018. It records that individuals, sessions, and presbyteries subsequently communicated concerns to Memorial and Missouri Presbytery. In October 2018, Memorial's Session and TE Greg Johnson asked Missouri Presbytery to investigate the church/session and Johnson's involvement/teaching.

## 2018–2019: Missouri Presbytery investigation

Primary judicial chronology:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/49th_pcaga_2022_vol02.pdf
https://pcahistory.org/pca/sjc/cases/2020-12_Speck_v_Missouri.pdf

Missouri Presbytery accepted the investigation/reference and in May 2019 approved an investigative report containing nine theological judgments. A complaint was filed; Missouri partially sustained it in October 2019 and undertook further work/reconsideration.

## 2019 General Assembly: Overture 28 / Warhurst debate

Official 47th GA Minutes:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/47th_pcaga_2019.pdf

Named floor roles:
- TE Sean Lucas — Overtures Committee chairman; presented the committee rationale.
- TE Steve Warhurst — presented the Minority Report.
- TE David Richter — point of order concerning germaneness of part of Warhurst's argument.
- TE Ewan Kennedy — point of order arguing the Minority Report addressed Revoice and was not germane; Moderator ruled it germane because Revoice was mentioned in the overture.

The minority substitute failed 210–967; the committee recommendation was then adopted by common consent.
