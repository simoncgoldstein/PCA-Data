# Revoice and the PCA — chronology, people, and church-court source map

Snapshot/research date: **2026-09-03**

This dossier treats Revoice as a major PCA event family spanning conference organization, a PCA congregation's hosting role, presbytery investigation, General Assembly actions, formal protests/minority reports, SJC litigation, the Human Sexuality Ad Interim Committee, and the eventual departure of Memorial Presbyterian Church and TE Greg Johnson from PCA jurisdiction.

The data model must keep those actions distinct. `Speaker at Revoice`, `host church officer`, `investigatory committee member`, `Warhurst protest signer`, `Human Sexuality committee member`, `Overture 37 minority-report signer`, and `SJC participant` are not interchangeable labels.

## 2017–2018 founding chronology from Missouri Presbytery's report

The Missouri report gives a detailed first-party/presbytery reconstruction:

- **August 29, 2017:** Nashville Statement published.
- **Late October / early November 2017:** Nate Collins contacts Stephen Moss with the idea for a conference responding to concerns experienced by the Side B community.
- **Late November 2017:** Moss, a member and former intern at Memorial Presbyterian Church, asks Memorial to host a conference described initially around Wes Hill and others.
- **December 14, 2017:** Memorial Session approves use of the church for the event by email. The Session understood itself initially as host/provider of space, not organizer or sponsor.
- **January 2018:** Collins and Moss begin inviting speakers. The report names **Wesley Hill, Eve Tushnet, and Nate Collins** as keynote speakers.
- **February 7, 2018:** Revoice website launches.
- **February 19, 2018:** Moss contacts TE Greg Johnson to arrange a meeting with Collins. The report says Johnson did not previously know Collins.
- **March 5, 2018:** the Revoice board is formally organized. Initial board: **Stephen Moss, Christina Gillam, Nate Collins**.
- **March 2018:** Greg Johnson joins/participates in the PCA Pastors & Elders Facebook group in an attempt to address questions about Memorial/Revoice.
- **May 28, 2018:** Johnson publishes a public defense/clarification in response to criticism.
- **June 5, 2018:** Memorial Session issues a congregational statement reaffirming its decision to host the event.
- **July 26–28, 2018:** first Revoice conference occurs at Memorial Presbyterian Church.

The report states that speaker selection beyond the initial proposal was made by Collins and Moss **without review or approval by Memorial's Session**. It also concludes that Memorial's relationship to Revoice deepened during the controversy: by the conference, the church was doing more than merely renting space and was encouraging members to volunteer, while Greg Johnson emerged publicly as a defender of Revoice as he understood it.

## Memorial Presbyterian Church role

Missouri Presbytery's report is nuanced and should be represented literally:

- Memorial initially understood itself as **host**, not sponsor/organizer.
- The Session approved the conference before it knew the full speaker roster/content.
- Collins and Moss selected the rest of the speakers independently of Session review.
- As criticism intensified, Johnson and the Session defended the decision to host.
- By the conference itself, the report says Memorial's involvement had deepened and church members were encouraged to volunteer.

The site should therefore avoid the oversimplified labels `Revoice organizer = Memorial` or `Memorial merely rented a room`; the primary report documents a changing relationship.

## 2018 Covenant Theological Seminary connection

The 46th GA minutes record that Covenant Seminary President **Mark Dalbey** addressed the Assembly because of perceived Seminary involvement. He stated that Covenant was neither hosting nor sponsoring Revoice and reaffirmed the Seminary's position on marriage and sexuality.

The Missouri report independently says Greg Johnson knew that **one featured Revoice speaker had previously spoken at Covenant Seminary**. An appendix reproducing Johnson's description of the planned event says presenters included **a Covenant Seminary professor**.

Contemporary reporting identifies that professor as **Dr. Jay Sklar**, then Old Testament professor/Vice President of Academics, who presented on Leviticus at Revoice 2018. This identity should be marked `strongly supported` until a preserved original 2018 Revoice program is added to the source tree. The formal 2018 GA record is definitive for the fact that Covenant's president publicly addressed perceived Seminary involvement; it is not evidence that Covenant Seminary institutionally sponsored Revoice.

A later 2019 Covenant statement said the Seminary did not endorse/promote Revoice and would have no faculty speaking at the 2019 conference.

## 2019 Missouri Presbytery judgments

Missouri Presbytery received the report and adopted a judicial judgment and theological judgments, along with recommendations. The committee said there was much to commend in Revoice's stated vision/work but also serious concerns and several errors arising from human weakness; it said it could not firmly judge those errors as striking at the vitals of religion.

Later in 2019 Missouri reconsidered/amended several theological judgments, and in January 2020 published an open letter summarizing its actions.

Primary Missouri publication index:
https://www.mopres.org/publications

The source tree should eventually preserve each adopted theological judgment as a separate structured record rather than reducing the report to `approved Revoice` or `condemned Revoice`.

## 2019 47th General Assembly

### Overture 28 / Nashville Statement context

The 47th GA debated an overture addressing homosexuality and the Nashville Statement. TE Steven Warhurst delivered the speech supporting an Overtures Committee minority report/expanded proposal.

### Kevin Twit protest

TE **Kevin Twit** filed a formal protest against the Assembly's allowing portions of Warhurst's speech to continue, alleging perceived intemperate language, especially judgments of Revoice speakers' motives.

Official minutes preserve the protest and **203 commissioner signers** on pp. 80–85. Presbyterian Polity independently summarizes the official count as 181 TEs and 22 REs.

## SJC / Missouri Presbytery litigation and Memorial departure

The 49th GA and subsequent SJC records preserve multiple cases concerning Missouri Presbytery's handling of Memorial/Greg Johnson.

SJC Case 2022-12 records that:
- Southeast Alabama Presbytery and Grace Presbytery later petitioned for original jurisdiction concerning alleged doctrinal errors of TE Greg Johnson.
- Memorial Presbyterian Church voted to withdraw from the PCA on **November 18, 2022**.
- Greg Johnson and two other TEs requested removal from Missouri Presbytery's rolls.
- Missouri Presbytery acknowledged the church's departure and the ministerial removals on **December 6, 2022**.
- The SJC consequently held that the PCA no longer had jurisdiction over Johnson for the pending petitions.

Primary decision:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/sjc/cases/2022-12_Petitions_re_Missouri_Presbytery.pdf

This creates a dated PCA-status trajectory rather than an inference from present-day Revoice activity.
