# Women serving in ministry / deaconess / female-deacon debates in the PCA

This dossier tracks a recurring PCA issue from the 2008 deaconess controversy through the 2016–2017 Women Serving in the Ministry of the Church study committee and into current AMR discussion. It must distinguish: (1) formal PCA constitutional position, (2) local-practice disputes, (3) study-committee participation, and (4) individual arguments for or against changes.

## 2008: deaconess / diaconal-ministry controversy

Official 36th GA Actions:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/ga/actions/36thGA_2008_Actions.pdf

Official 36th GA Minutes:
https://pcahistory.org/pca/ga/36th_pcaga_2008.pdf

PCA Stated Clerk summary:
https://www.pcahistory.org/pca/clerk/2008.html

The 36th GA received several actions concerning women's participation in diaconal/mercy ministry:

- Philadelphia Presbytery, Overture 9, asked for a study committee on biblical evidence concerning women in mercy ministry.
- Western Canada, Overture 15, seconded that request.
- Northern California, Communication 2, was related.
- Rocky Mountain, Overture 17, sought an expanded committee scope concerning where women may serve, ordained or unordained.
- Central Georgia, Overture 19, opposed a study committee and argued that commissioning persons for diaconal ministry was unwarranted.

The Overtures Committee majority recommended no study committee. A minority report supported a study committee limited to women's roles in diaconal ministry. After extended debate the minority report failed and the majority report prevailed by approximately 60–40.

The Stated Clerk's summary explicitly notes the PCA BCO did not allow women to be ordained as deacons but did allow Sessions to appoint godly women to assist the diaconate under BCO 9-7.

## SJC 2008-01: Crossroads Community Church Session v. Philadelphia Presbytery

Primary decision:
https://pcahistory.org/pca/sjc/cases/2008-01_Crossroads_v_Philadelphia.pdf

SJC index/summary:
https://pcahistory.org/pca/sjc/2005-2014.html

The complaint concerned Philadelphia Presbytery's licensing/ordination of a man who differed with the BCO regarding women's eligibility for the office of deacon but pledged to conduct ministry according to the PCA form of government.

The SJC denied the complaints and stated that the BCO was conclusive that women could not be ordained to the office of deacon. The published vote was 10 concur, 3 dissent, 10 absent. Named SJC votes can be normalized as formal judicial-action evidence, not as broad ideological classification.

## Subsequent Philadelphia Presbytery / local-practice litigation

Later SJC cases, including Reese & Bech v. Philadelphia Presbytery, preserve disputes over churches using mixed male/female diaconal bodies, not ordaining any member, and describing men and women as equal partners in diaconal ministry. These cases are valuable for mapping specific local-practice models and judicial responses.

## 2016: formation of Women Serving in the Ministry of the Church committee

The 44th General Assembly approved formation of a study committee on women serving in church ministry consistent with PCA standards. National Partnership correspondence from 2016 shows James Kessler strongly supported formation of the committee and organized NP members to be present for the relevant Assembly action.

The source archive also contains a Cal Boroughs note describing the contemplated subjects as ordination, the nature/function of the diaconal office, and clarification regarding deacons/deaconesses.
